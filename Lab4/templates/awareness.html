🎣 Gotcha — this was a phishing simulation.

If this had been a real attack, the credentials you just typed would now be in an attacker's hands. Because this is a lab, they were only written to a local log file to show how capture works — and we hope you used a fake password like the page asked.

Red flags you could have caught

  • The URL wasn't the real site. A page served from 127.0.0.1 (or a look-alike domain) is not {{ brand }}. Always check the address bar before typing a password.
  • An unexpected "urgent" prompt. Phishing lures push you to act fast — "verify now", "your account will be locked".
  • A generic greeting ("Dear user") instead of your name.
  • No verified sender. The email that linked here came from a look-alike address, not an official domain.
  • The banner literally said "phishing simulation". 🙂

How to protect yourself

  • Navigate to sites yourself instead of clicking links in emails.
  • Use a password manager — it won't autofill on a look-alike domain.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA) / passkeys.
  • Report suspicious messages to your IT/security team.
